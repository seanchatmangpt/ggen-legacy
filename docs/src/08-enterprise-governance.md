# Enterprise Architecture and Governance

## Operating model

The architecture preserves the four Ross–Weill–Robertson operating models: Diversification, Coordination, Replication, and Unification. A customer profile declares standardization and integration needs rather than forcing every enterprise into one topology.

Evidence-Bearing Self-Governance is a ggen extension. The system may observe, diagnose, plan, construct, emit intent, verify, receipt, and replay. It may not directly actuate consequential state or certify itself.

## TOGAF and ArchiMate

The canonical enterprise graph supports the complete TOGAF ADM lifecycle: Preliminary, Phases A–H, and Requirements Management. It records principles, stakeholders, baseline, target, transition architectures, capabilities, gaps, work packages, risks, controls, and decisions.

ArchiMate and executive diagrams are projections from the graph, not parallel authority. ABBs describe required behavior. SBBs satisfy ABBs at an exact coordinate. GBBs, bblocks, packs, and profiles provide the reusable manufacturing calculus.

## Portfolio governance

Modernization investment is governed by capability closure, operational risk, evidence debt, queue time, WIP, and retirement economics. Little’s Law disciplines work in process. Conway’s Law is treated as an architecture constraint: organization and ownership must align with intended module and authority boundaries.

## RACI separation

Product owns scope; Enterprise Architecture owns admitted structure; repository teams own implementation; Security owns control review; independent verifiers own evidence decisions; Release Authority owns release; Sunset Authority owns retirement. No actor both performs consequential mutation and independently certifies it.
